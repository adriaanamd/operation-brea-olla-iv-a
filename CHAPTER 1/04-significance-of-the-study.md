# Significance of the Study

This section explains how the report may be useful while keeping its claims within the limits of a single organized webinar and its available evidence.

## Students and beginners

The report can give beginners a concrete account of how AI-assisted coding may be connected to testing practice. The emphasis on requirements, executable tests, review, and revision may help readers see why generated code still needs human judgment. The report does not promise that reading it or attending the webinar produces programming competence. Its value for students is more modest: it records an example of how a beginner-oriented learning activity attempted to introduce a difficult and fast-changing topic.

## Aspiring software development engineers in test

For learners who are considering software testing or SDET-related work, the report brings together concepts that are often learned separately: test design, automation, code review, debugging, AI-assisted development, and communication of evidence. It may help them identify the difference between producing code and checking whether code meets a requirement. The report also makes unresolved implementation details visible, which is useful when deciding what evidence a future training activity should preserve.

## Faculty and computing educators

Faculty members may use the report as a small case account of teaching AI-assisted TDD outside a full semester course. The findings may help educators compare intended objectives with participant perceptions and identify where a short webinar needs more examples, guided practice, or follow-up assessment. The report should not be used as proof that one delivery format or tool is generally effective.

## Organizers and facilitators

For the organizing team, the report provides a structured record of planning evidence, delivery artifacts, participant feedback, and unresolved documentation gaps. This can support later decisions about program design, timing, technical rehearsal, collection of attendance evidence, privacy safeguards, and post-event evaluation. These uses depend on verifying the missing event details identified in the scope and methodology chapters.

## Future webinar and training activities

Future activities may use the report's evidence map to improve their own documentation. In particular, later organizers can preserve the final objectives, program sequence, presenter responsibilities, demonstration repository, test output, attendance basis, evaluation instrument, and analysis plan in one traceable package. A stronger follow-up study could also include a pre-test, a post-test, a performance task, or delayed evidence of transfer if the goal is to study learning rather than reaction.

## Contribution of the report

The report's main contribution is a transparent account of one educational activity at the intersection of AI-assisted development and software testing. It adds value by separating what was reported by participants, what is visible in supplied artifacts, what is inferred from those records, and what remains unknown. That distinction protects readers from treating a small post-event evaluation as a causal study and gives later work a clearer basis for improvement.
