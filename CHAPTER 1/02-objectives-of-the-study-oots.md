# Objectives of the Study

This section states what the report seeks to document and examine about the organized webinar titled "CodeCrafters: Tech Beyond the Screen." The objectives separate instructional aims from evaluation aims so that the activity's intended learning focus is not confused with the evidence collected after the session.

## General objective

The general objective of this report is to document the planning and implementation of "CodeCrafters: Tech Beyond the Screen" and to examine the participant-evaluation evidence available for the webinar on AI-assisted test-driven development for beginners.

## Specific instructional objectives

The webinar was intended to:

1. introduce beginners to the purpose of software testing and the basic idea of test-driven development;
2. explain the red-green-refactor cycle through a small, accessible example;
3. show how an AI coding assistant may support selected coding or testing tasks while leaving review and validation with the developer;
4. connect generated or modified code with executable tests and observable feedback; and
5. encourage beginners to treat AI output as material to inspect, test, and revise rather than as an unquestionable solution.

## Specific evaluation objectives

The report also aims to:

1. describe the documented planning and delivery context of the webinar;
2. identify the participant and attendance evidence available in the supplied records without exposing personal identifiers;
3. summarize participant ratings of the session's objectives, relevance, clarity, organization, technical delivery, overall experience, and satisfaction;
4. examine open-ended comments for reported learning value, liked features, suggested improvements, and other feedback; and
5. determine which conclusions are supported by the available artifacts and which remain unresolved.

These evaluation objectives concern participant perceptions and documented implementation. They do not claim that the webinar caused learning, changed behavior, improved coding performance, or produced durable TDD competence. Those claims would require stronger evidence than a post-event evaluation alone.
