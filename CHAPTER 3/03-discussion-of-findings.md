# Discussion of findings

The results describe a favorable immediate response to the webinar, but they do not establish learning gains. The normalized primary-item composite mean was 4.6598, and the two separate summary items also had high agreement rates. The pattern was consistent across the item set: every primary item had a mean above 4.5, while the lowest agreement rate, 92.31% for engagement and interactivity, still represented a strong majority of responses. These results indicate that respondents generally evaluated the session positively within the context of this event and respondent group.

The highest-rated primary dimension was the clarity and organization of the presentation materials (mean = 4.7308). This result fits the qualitative coding, where explanation and clarity received 17 assignments from 17 respondents and demonstration received 11 assignments from 11 respondents. The agreement between the rating and comment streams supports an interpretation that presentation structure and visible examples were noticed by participants. It does not show that the materials independently produced the ratings, because the evaluation was collected after the session and did not isolate individual instructional components.

Speaker clarity received the lowest primary-item mean at 4.5577, although 96.15% of respondents still selected 4 or 5. The clarity-by-overall-rating cross-tab showed that 26 of the 31 respondents who selected the strongest clarity category also selected an overall rating of 5. The corresponding Spearman association was 0.5459 across 52 complete cases. This is a descriptive positive association, not a causal estimate. It may reflect a relationship between delivery clarity and overall impressions, but it may also reflect shared response tendencies or other unmeasured features of the session.

The open-ended responses add content to the numerical pattern. AI use was the most frequent substantive code, with 36 assignments across 25 respondents. Software development and TDD or software testing followed with 22 assignments across 21 respondents and 21 assignments across 17 respondents, respectively. These comments are consistent with the webinar's human-guided view of AI-assisted development. Research on code-generation tools describes their usefulness while also documenting difficulties involving comprehension, editing, debugging, and security (Vaithilingam et al., 2022; Pearce et al., 2022). The comments about AI use and testing therefore fit the session's reported instructional emphasis, but they remain self-reported takeaways rather than evidence that respondents could independently perform those practices.

The TDD and testing comments also fit the framework's treatment of red-green-refactor as a feedback routine. Research has linked TDD with program design, software quality, flow, and developer experience, while also showing that its value depends on implementation conditions and developer context (Agha et al., 2023; Calais & Franzini, 2023). In this evaluation, the coded comments show that testing and development concepts were memorable to some respondents. They do not show durable skill, transfer to new tasks, or a causal change in competence.

The improvement codes qualify the favorable ratings. More examples had 7 assignments across 7 respondents, more interaction had 6 assignments across 5 respondents, and more depth had 4 assignments across 4 respondents. Technical-quality comments were less frequent, with 2 assignments across 2 respondents. This combination matters: respondents could rate the session highly and still identify a need for more practice, participation, or technical refinement. Research on generative AI in computing education similarly describes opportunities for novice learners alongside new demands on teaching, assessment, and responsible use (Becker et al., 2023; Denny et al., 2024; Kazemitabaar et al., 2023). The present findings support that balanced reading rather than a claim that high satisfaction means that all instructional needs were met.

The scale-direction correction should remain a data-quality decision rather than a substantive finding. Two rows met the pre-specified flag rule, and each of their 13 primary values changed from 1 to 5. As a result, every primary-item mean increased by 0.1538 in the sensitivity table. The correction is transparent and consistent with the positive summary ratings, but it is still an analyst judgment. Reporting both the audit and the sensitivity table allows readers to see how much the decision affected the descriptive results.

Taken together, the quantitative and qualitative strands support a bounded conclusion. Participants reported a positive immediate experience, particularly in relation to materials, organization, relevance, and technical adequacy. Their comments indicate that AI-assisted development, software development, testing, explanations, and demonstrations were salient. At the same time, requests for more examples, interaction, and depth show that positive reception should not be treated as proof of objective learning or as evidence that the webinar caused later performance changes.
