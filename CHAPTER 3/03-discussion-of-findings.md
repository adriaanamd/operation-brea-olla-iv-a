# Discussion of findings

The Python-generated CRISP-DM outputs support a bounded interpretation of the webinar evaluation. The evidence shows a favorable immediate response among the 52 available respondents, but it does not establish objective learning, later transfer, or causal impact. The discussion therefore treats the numeric outputs as measures of reported perception and the coded outputs as measures of reported feedback.

## Data-quality result: a valid but deliberately bounded analytic set

The data-understanding and evaluation outputs show 52 respondent rows, 24 source fields, and valid values in all 15 analyzed rating fields. No rating cells were missing or out of range, and no duplicate generated respondent IDs were present. Two duplicate timestamps were retained as a diagnostic condition because timestamps were not used as respondent identifiers. This supports using all 52 rows for descriptive analysis, while the absence of a verified attendance denominator prevents a response-rate or attendee-level inference. The relevant records are [`crispdm_data_profile.csv`](../ANALYSIS/outputs/tables/crispdm_data_profile.csv), [`crispdm_validation_summary.csv`](../ANALYSIS/outputs/tables/crispdm_validation_summary.csv), and [`data_quality_summary.csv`](../ANALYSIS/outputs/tables/data_quality_summary.csv).

The preparation stage also identified two scale-direction cases. Each case had all 13 primary matrix values recorded as `1` while both separate summary ratings were at least `4`. The pipeline changed only those 26 primary cells from `1` to `5` and retained overall rating and satisfaction as anchors. The normalization sensitivity output shows the same **0.1538-point increase** for every primary-item mean, so the correction changed the level of the results but not the ranking of the items. This makes the decision consequential enough to report, but transparent enough to audit. It remains an analyst decision rather than proof of what either respondent intended. See [`normalization_audit.csv`](../ANALYSIS/outputs/tables/normalization_audit.csv) and [`crispdm_normalization_sensitivity.csv`](../ANALYSIS/outputs/tables/crispdm_normalization_sensitivity.csv).

## Quantitative result: high ratings with limited separation

The normalized 13-item composite had a mean of **4.6598**, median of **4.9231**, and SD of **0.4390**. The high median relative to the mean indicates that responses were concentrated near the upper end, with a small number of lower evaluations pulling the mean downward. The composite should be read as a descriptive aggregation of the 13 statements, not as a validated psychometric scale. Overall satisfaction was similarly favorable: its mean was **4.6731**, with **36 of 52** respondents selecting 5 and **98.08%** selecting 4 or 5. The overall session-rating mean was **4.6154**, with **33 of 52** selecting 5 and the same **98.08%** agreement rate. These outputs indicate positive reception, not demonstrated learning.

The item-level output shows a narrow range of means, from **4.5577** for speaker clarity to **4.7308** for presentation materials. Presentation materials had the highest top-box rate (**75.00%**) and a bootstrap mean interval of **4.5962–4.8654**. Speaker clarity had the lowest mean and top-box rate (**59.62%**) and an interval of **4.4038–4.7115**. Because these intervals overlap, the ranking is useful for identifying relative emphasis but should not be treated as evidence of a statistically established difference between materials and speaker clarity. The lowest agreement percentage belonged to engagement and interactivity (**92.31%**, Wilson interval **81.83%–96.97%**), which is a more useful improvement signal than the small difference between the highest and lowest means. The complete estimates are in [`crispdm_quantitative_metrics.csv`](../ANALYSIS/outputs/tables/crispdm_quantitative_metrics.csv).

This pattern is consistent with a ceiling effect: most respondents selected 4 or 5, but fewer selected 5 consistently. The distinction between agreement and top-box responses is important for the decision handoff. A session can be broadly accepted while still leaving room to improve the proportion of respondents who describe it as excellent. The item-means, response-distribution, composite, and summary-rating figures in [`ANALYSIS/outputs/figures/`](../ANALYSIS/outputs/figures/) make that concentration visible.

## Speaker clarity: a useful association, not a causal result

The cross-tabulation provides a more specific pattern than the overall means. Among the 31 respondents who selected the strongest speaker-clarity category, **26 (83.87%)** also selected an overall session rating of 5. Among the 19 respondents selecting clarity level 4, **7 (36.84%)** selected an overall rating of 5; the two respondents at clarity level 3 selected neither a 5 overall rating. The descriptive Spearman association was **rho = 0.5459** across 52 complete cases. This suggests that delivery clarity and overall evaluation moved together in this respondent set, but it cannot show that clarity caused the overall rating. Shared response style, session content, or other unmeasured factors could explain part of the association. The association and cross-tab outputs are [`crispdm_association_summary.csv`](../ANALYSIS/outputs/tables/crispdm_association_summary.csv) and [`crispdm_speaker_clarity_overall_crosstab.csv`](../ANALYSIS/outputs/tables/crispdm_speaker_clarity_overall_crosstab.csv).

## Qualitative result: the prompt structure changes the interpretation

Thematic analysis adds information that the aggregate ratings cannot provide. Across the coded decisions, AI use was the most frequent substantive theme (**36 assignments across 25 respondents; 48.08% respondent coverage**), followed by software development (**22 assignments across 21 respondents; 40.38%**) and TDD or software testing (**21 assignments across 17 respondents; 32.69%**). These frequencies describe what respondents reported in their answers; they do not verify that respondents could independently perform the practices.

The prompt-by-theme output shows that the learning-value responses concentrated on AI use (**22 assignments**), software development (**21**), and TDD/testing (**14**). The liked-most responses were more delivery-oriented: explanation and clarity had **17 assignments**, AI use **14**, and demonstrations **11**. The improvements prompt produced **7** requests for more examples, **5** for more interaction, and **3** for more depth. Additional comments were dominated by `NO_SUBSTANTIVE_RESPONSE` (**42 assignments**), which means that this prompt contributes less usable explanatory evidence than the learning-value, liked-most, and improvements prompts. These prompt-level differences are visible in [`crispdm_theme_by_prompt.csv`](../ANALYSIS/outputs/tables/crispdm_theme_by_prompt.csv) and [`theme_by_prompt_heatmap.png`](../ANALYSIS/outputs/figures/theme_by_prompt_heatmap.png).

The qualitative pattern is compatible with research describing AI-assisted programming as useful but dependent on human comprehension, checking, editing, debugging, and security awareness (Vaithilingam et al., 2022; Pearce et al., 2022). It also fits research that treats TDD as a feedback and design practice whose value depends on implementation conditions rather than as an automatic learning outcome (Agha et al., 2023; Calais & Franzini, 2023). In this dataset, the safest conclusion is that AI-assisted development and testing were salient reported takeaways—not that the webinar produced durable competence.

## Mixed-methods convergence and divergence

The CRISP-DM evaluation stage supports convergence in three areas. First, presentation materials had the highest quantitative mean (**4.7308**), while explanation and clarity was the most frequent valued-delivery theme (**17 assignments across 17 respondents**). Second, the session's technical and organizational items were highly rated, and respondents described demonstrations and practical relevance as valued features. Third, both the ratings and the open-ended responses point toward a generally favorable immediate experience.

The strands also diverged in a useful way. Quantitative ratings were positive even though the improvements prompt contained requests for more examples, interaction, and depth. Engagement had the lowest agreement rate (**92.31%**) despite its high mean (**4.6154**), while the coded improvement themes identified more practice and participation as the clearest next-step requests. This is not a contradiction: satisfaction measures the experienced session, whereas improvement comments identify what respondents would add to a future session. The mixed-methods evidence matrix formalizes this distinction in [`mixed_methods_evidence_matrix.csv`](../ANALYSIS/outputs/tables/mixed_methods_evidence_matrix.csv).

## From Python output to decision handoff

The deployment stage of the CRISP-DM workflow converts the findings into three bounded actions. First, retain the clear organization and presentation materials because they were the strongest quantitative dimensions. Second, add guided examples, interactive debugging, or test-writing practice because the prompt-specific themes identified more examples and more interaction as the most frequent improvement requests. Third, protect measurement quality by keeping the Likert direction visually explicit and preserving the normalization audit. These are evidence-informed design cues for the next evaluation cycle, not tested interventions. The generated handoff is recorded in [`crispdm_decision_prompts.csv`](../ANALYSIS/outputs/tables/crispdm_decision_prompts.csv).

Taken together, the data support the conclusion that participants reported a positive immediate experience and that AI-assisted development, software development, testing, explanations, and demonstrations were salient. They also support a specific improvement agenda: preserve structure, increase active practice, and strengthen measurement quality. They do not support claims of objective learning, retention, transfer, or causal webinar effects.
