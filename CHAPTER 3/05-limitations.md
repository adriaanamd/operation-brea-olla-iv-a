# Limitations

Several limitations constrain the interpretation of the findings.

## Design and sampling

The evidence came from one webinar and 52 available evaluation responses. The supplied materials did not provide a verified attendance denominator, registration count, or response rate. The analysis therefore describes the available respondent group rather than all registrants or attendees. The sampling basis was availability-based, and the results should not be generalized to other webinars, institutions, or learner populations without additional evidence.

The institution field also contained capitalization and spelling variants, including several variants of Gordon College. Because the current pipeline reports the field as recorded rather than harmonizing institution labels, the institutional profile is descriptive only. It should not be used to estimate institutional participation without a documented normalization rule.

## Measurement and interpretation

The evaluation was collected after the activity and relied on self-report. It measured perceived clarity, relevance, organization, engagement, usefulness, technical adequacy, overall rating, and satisfaction. It did not measure objective competence, delayed retention, transfer to a new task, or causal learning effects. The high ratings also suggest a possible ceiling effect: most responses were 4 or 5, leaving limited variation for distinguishing stronger and weaker experiences.

The 13 primary statements were treated as a descriptive Likert set. The analysis reported means, standard deviations, distributions, agreement rates, top-box rates, and uncertainty intervals for communication purposes, but the instrument was not psychometrically validated and the items were not established as a single latent scale. The composite mean should therefore not be interpreted as a validated learning or satisfaction index. The bootstrap and Wilson intervals quantify sampling variability under their respective descriptive procedures; they do not correct for availability sampling or establish population inference.

Two response-pattern cases were normalized because all 13 primary items were recorded as 1 while both summary ratings were positive. The correction was transparent and limited to the primary matrix, but it remains an analyst decision. The sensitivity output shows that the rule increased every primary-item mean by 0.1538. Readers should consider the raw and normalized summaries together, especially if the evaluation instrument is reused.

## Qualitative coding

The open-ended responses were coded by one analyst. No second coder or inter-rater agreement estimate was available. Multiple codes could be assigned to one answer, so assignment counts are not mutually exclusive respondent counts. The coding record also contained 99 `NO_SUBSTANTIVE_RESPONSE` assignments involving 47 respondents across the four prompts. This limited the depth of some qualitative comparisons, particularly for improvement and additional-comment fields.

The themes represent what respondents reported in their answers. They do not verify that respondents acquired the reported knowledge or could apply it independently. The qualitative findings should therefore be used to identify perceived value and requested improvements, not to estimate learning outcomes.

The supplied materials did not include a verified attendance record, official program, recording, complete slide deck, repository or test-execution log, or platform diagnostics.
