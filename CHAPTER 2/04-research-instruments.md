# Research instruments

The supplied Google Forms-style export contained four evidence groups:

1. consent to the collection and processing of personal information;
2. consent to documentation;
3. 13 five-point experience statements; and
4. two separate five-point summary questions plus four open-ended prompts.

The 13 primary statements addressed objective clarity, topic relevance, ease of understanding, speaker clarity, material organization, examples and discussions, engagement, reinforcement activities, usefulness, critical thinking, organization, technical quality, and meeting expectations. The response anchors were encoded in the export as values such as `5 – Strongly Agree`; the pipeline extracted the leading integer on the 1–5 scale.

The two summary questions asked for an overall session rating and satisfaction. They were analyzed separately because they are conceptually broader than the 13 experience statements and because their positive values served as internal consistency anchors when two rows showed an implausible all-1 pattern on the primary items.

The open-ended prompts asked what respondents learned, what they liked most, what could be improved, and whether they had additional comments or suggestions. The instrument was treated as an evaluation form, not as a validated psychometric scale. No reliability coefficient, factor structure, or construct-validity claim was estimated.
